==================================================
OPAMP DESIGN GUIDANCE
==================================================

The circuit under design is an operational amplifier. Use the actual
optimization variables, bounds, constraints, measured context, and numerical
targets supplied above. Do not assume any topology element or tunable parameter
that is not present in the runtime parameter space.

==================================================
1. CANONICAL PERFORMANCE SEMANTICS
==================================================

low_frequency_open_loop_gain_db is the low-frequency magnitude, in dB, of the
formal open-loop gain extracted from the validated return ratio. When its
runtime target uses a lower-bound operator, treat it as a quality constraint.

unity_gain_frequency_hz is the frequency, in Hz, of the first valid descending
0-dB crossing of the formal Mode-2 return-ratio open-loop gain. Treat its
runtime lower-bound target as the primary speed objective: improve it when it
is violated, but do not spend gain or stability margin that is needed to meet
their constraints.

phase_margin_deg is the formal loop-gain phase margin, in degrees, evaluated
at that selected descending unity-gain crossing. Treat its runtime lower-bound
target as a stability constraint. A benchmark threshold near ninety degrees is
a runtime-defined stability guard band, not an instruction to maximize phase
margin without limit.

Read every numerical threshold from target_specs. No example threshold in
this guidance overrides the runtime target.

==================================================
2. CONSTRAINT-FIRST TARGET REASONING
==================================================

Consider all three metrics jointly. First protect or recover the lower-bound
quality and stability constraints. Then move the primary speed metric toward
its threshold while retaining enough margin on those constraints. Do not
maximize gain, speed, and phase margin independently, and do not pursue an
already satisfied metric far beyond its threshold while another target remains
violated.

Use the best-known measured anchor as the primary local reference. Prefer a
moderate, evidence-supported step when the anchor is near joint feasibility.

==================================================
3. COUPLED PARAMETER REASONING
==================================================

Transistor widths and lengths can jointly affect transconductance, output
resistance, capacitance, poles, and bias distribution. Bias variables can
shift operating points and bandwidth while also changing gain and stability.
These effects are topology-dependent and coupled; do not impose a universal
monotonic rule such as wider always means faster or longer always means more
stable.

Infer directions from all supplied measured neighbors and from the actual
runtime variable names. Preserve explicit matching, ratio, and shared-parameter
constraints. Do not change fixed supplies, common-mode conditions, loads,
feedback construction, or testbench settings unless they explicitly appear as
optimization variables.
